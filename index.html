🏁 LeeOnTrack League Racing

Racing Together. Improving Together.

LeeOnTrack League Racing was created for like-minded sim racers who want to enjoy competitive racing while improving their race craft and helping others improve theirs.

The league is specifically aimed at drivers with an iRating under 2000. You don’t need to be an alien, and you don’t need to be the fastest driver on the grid. What matters to us is that you want to learn, improve and race fairly.

A League for Developing Drivers

We know that racing in public sessions can sometimes be intimidating, especially when you’re still developing your race craft.

LeeOnTrack gives drivers a place where they can gain experience racing regularly with the same group of people.

It’s an opportunity to work on:

  • Clean overtaking and defending
  • Racing safely side-by-side
  • Race starts and first-lap awareness
  • Consistency and car control
  • Pit stops and race strategy
  • Dealing with traffic and blue flags
  • Learning when to attack — and when to be patient

Most importantly, it’s about gaining real racing experience in a friendly environment.

Help Each Other Improve

The league isn’t just about your own results.

We encourage drivers to share advice, give constructive feedback and help each other improve.

If someone makes a mistake, we’d rather help them understand what happened than simply criticise them.

More experienced drivers within the league are encouraged to pass on what they’ve learned, while newer drivers should never be afraid to ask questions.

We all improve by racing together.

Competitive — But Friendly

We still want proper racing.

There are positions to fight for, races to win and championships to compete for.

But winning should never come at the expense of respect for another driver.

We want drivers to finish a race thinking:

“That was a great battle.”

Whether they finished 1st, 10th or last.

Under 2000 iRating

LeeOnTrack League Racing is designed primarily for drivers with an iRating below 2000.

This helps create a grid where developing and intermediate racers can compete against drivers of broadly similar ability.

It isn’t about judging anyone by their iRating.

It’s about creating the right environment for drivers to build confidence, improve their race craft and become better racers.

Be Part of the Community

Discord and communication are a big part of the league.

Talk to the other drivers.

Share setups and advice.

Help somebody who’s struggling with a track.

Congratulate somebody after a good battle.

And if something goes wrong on track, talk about it respectfully and learn from it.

The LeeOnTrack Philosophy

You don’t have to be the fastest.

You don’t have to win.

You just need to want to race fairly, improve your driving and help create enjoyable racing for everyone on the grid.

Race. Learn. Improve. Together.

Code of Conduct

🏁 LeeOnTrack League Racing

Driver Code of Conduct

Our league is about clean, competitive and enjoyable racing.

We all make mistakes, and accidents will happen. What we expect from every driver is respect, common sense and fair racing.

By taking part in a LeeOnTrack League race, drivers agree to follow this Racing Code.

General Driving Standards
  • Race hard, but race fairly.
  • Treat other drivers with respect both on and off the track.
  • Deliberately hitting, blocking, wrecking or retaliating against another driver is not acceptable.
  • Drivers must make reasonable efforts to avoid contact.
  • If you make a mistake that causes another driver to lose a position, giving the position back is encouraged where it can be done safely.
  • Remember: the race cannot be won on Lap 1, but it can certainly be lost there.
Race Starts & Lap 1

Extra care is expected at the start of every race.

  • Give other cars racing room.
  • Expect cars to be alongside you into the opening corners.
  • Avoid desperate moves into Turn 1.
  • Do not force another driver off the circuit.
  • Cold tyres, heavy fuel and close racing mean braking distances may be longer than expected.

Lap 1 is about getting everyone into the race — not winning it at the first corner.

Racing Alongside Another Car

When cars are genuinely racing side-by-side:

  • Both drivers should leave sufficient racing room.
  • Do not squeeze another car off the track.
  • Do not deliberately make contact to complete an overtake.
  • Be aware of your spotter and surroundings.
  • If another car has established meaningful overlap, respect that the car is there.

Clean side-by-side racing is what we want to see.

Braking & Dive Bombs

The overtaking driver is responsible for attempting a safe pass.

A driver should not:

  • Launch from an unrealistic distance into a corner.
  • Use another car to slow themselves down.
  • Force another driver off the circuit because they have entered the corner too quickly.
  • Make a move where contact is the only realistic way of completing the pass.

Being able to reach the inside of a corner does not automatically mean the move was safe.

Defending

Drivers are allowed to defend their position, but it must be fair and predictable.

  • Do not weave repeatedly to stop another driver passing.
  • Do not move under braking in reaction to the car behind.
  • Once committed to a defensive line, be predictable.
  • Leave racing room when another car is legitimately alongside.

Aggressive defending is allowed. Dangerous blocking is not.

Blue Flags & Lapped Cars

Blue flags in iRacing are informational.

When being lapped

  • Remain predictable.
  • Do not suddenly change direction to get out of the way.
  • Do not defend your position against a car that is lapping you.
  • Where reasonably possible, help facilitate the pass at a safe location.
  • Avoid unnecessarily holding up cars fighting for lead-lap positions.

The faster driver must also behave responsibly

  • The lead-lap car does not automatically own the corner because the other car is a lap down.
  • The overtaking driver remains responsible for completing the pass safely.
  • Do not force a lapped car off the circuit or cause contact expecting them simply to disappear.

Simple rule:

Lapped car — be predictable and facilitate the pass.

Faster car — make the pass safely.

Rejoining the Track

If you spin, crash or leave the circuit:

Do not immediately drive back onto the racing line.

Before rejoining:

  • Check your relative/spotter.
  • Wait for approaching traffic where necessary.
  • Rejoin in a controlled and predictable manner.
  • Do not reverse across the racing line.
  • Do not perform a dangerous U-turn in front of approaching cars.

A bad rejoin can turn one driver’s mistake into a multi-car accident.

Safe rejoining takes priority over getting back into the race quickly.

Yellow Flags & Incidents Ahead

When approaching an accident:

  • Be prepared to slow down.
  • Watch for cars stopped or rejoining.
  • Do not blindly drive through an incident at full speed.
  • Give yourself enough room to react.

Drivers involved in an incident should remain as predictable as possible.

Overtaking

When attempting an overtake:

  • Make your intentions clear.
  • Be under control when entering the braking zone.
  • Leave room for the other driver where appropriate.
  • Do not rely on contact to complete the move.
  • Consider whether the move can realistically be completed safely.

Both drivers have a responsibility to avoid unnecessary contact.

Pit Entry & Exit

Drivers must:

  • Enter the pits safely and predictably.
  • Avoid suddenly crossing the circuit to make pit entry.
  • Respect the pit-lane speed limit.
  • Stay within the pit exit procedure.
  • Be aware of cars approaching at racing speed when leaving the pits.

Where possible, communicate your intention to pit if another driver is immediately behind you.

Damaged Cars

If your car is badly damaged:

  • Stay off the racing line where reasonably possible.
  • Do not attempt to race normally if the car cannot be controlled safely.
  • Allow faster traffic to pass.
  • Tow or return to the pits if continuing would create an unnecessary danger.
Voice & Discord Conduct

Friendly chat and banter are part of the league, but respect comes first.

  • No personal abuse or bullying.
  • Do not start arguments over voice chat during the race.
  • Do not repeatedly shout at another driver following an incident.
  • If there is a disagreement, finish the race first and discuss it afterwards.
  • Admin decisions must be respected.

Race first. Discuss incidents afterwards.

Retaliation & Deliberate Wrecking

There is zero tolerance for deliberate retaliation.

This includes:

  • Intentionally wrecking another driver.
  • Brake-checking another driver.
  • Waiting for another driver in order to hit them.
  • Deliberately pushing someone off the circuit.
  • Using the car as retaliation for an earlier incident.

Serious or deliberate incidents may result in immediate removal from the event or league.

Incident Reviews

Where an incident is reported, league admins may review available replay footage.

Admin will consider:

  • Driver positioning.
  • Racing overlap.
  • Braking points.
  • Whether sufficient racing room was provided.
  • Whether contact was avoidable.
  • Whether either driver behaved recklessly.
  • Whether the incident appears deliberate.

Drivers are expected to provide their side calmly and respectfully.

An accident does not automatically mean somebody must be punished.

Penalties

Depending on the seriousness or frequency of an offence, league administration may use:

  • Verbal/written warning.
  • Position or points penalty.
  • Post-race time penalty.
  • Qualifying restriction.
  • Start-from-the-back penalty.
  • Drive-through or other race penalty where appropriate.
  • Race suspension.
  • Removal from the league for serious or repeated misconduct.

Repeated incidents may result in stronger penalties even where an individual incident would normally receive only a warning.

The LeeOnTrack Standard

We want drivers of different abilities to feel welcome racing with us.

You don’t have to be the fastest driver.

You do have to race with respect.

Race hard. Race fair. Leave room. Have fun.
